# Held-Out Adversarial Evaluation — Protocol and Amendments

**Status:** Historical protocol with disclosed amendments. Repository history records
the initial protocol before the original held-out CSVs, but this chronology does not make
the self-authored generator or oracle independent. Amendment v2 changed two generator
details and regenerated the original census. Amendment v3 adds a seed-paired three-defence
comparison after late review; it does not replace the original 20-seed PerceptFence
sensitivity census.

**Purpose.** The original PerceptFence benchmark is *circular*: the same author wrote the
11 fixtures, their expected outcomes, and the deterministic rules, yielding an
uninformative "full_guard 11/11 vs baseline 0/11." This protocol replaces that with a
deterministic adversarial census whose cases are generated by self-authored templates
informed by a published evasion taxonomy, whose ground-truth labels are construction
facts (a known payload was injected), and whose exposure oracle follows a separate code
path from the redactor. The generator, redactor, and oracle nevertheless share authorship,
concepts, and some normalization assumptions. The census is designed to expose an honest
**recall < 1.0** result with an explicit, characterized coverage boundary.

## 1. Threat & unit of evaluation
For each generated case, a known **synthetic** sensitive payload `P` (credential, US SSN,
payment-card number, email, or screen-visible injection instruction) is embedded — after
an evasion transform — into a benign carrier containing benign task-critical tokens.
Ground truth: `P` is present (a construction fact, not a judgment). A defense **neutralizes**
`P` if `P` is *not recoverable* from the defense's output (Section 4). Benign tokens must
survive (reported separately as a literal benign-token removal proxy).

All payloads are synthetic and follow public *format* catalogs only (e.g., the standard
`4111…` Visa test-card prefix, RFC-5322 e-mail grammar, NANP SSN area/group ranges). No
real secrets, real PII, or real captures are used.

## 2. Literature-informed evasion taxonomy
Operators are informed by published sources and their provenance is recorded in
`taxonomy.py`. The concrete templates, parameter ranges, and coverage labels are
self-authored. The resulting census is therefore a deterministic synthetic stress test,
not an independent red-team corpus or estimate of a deployment distribution.

| # | Family | Source basis | Redactor coverage (declared a priori) |
|---|--------|--------------|----------------------------------------|
| 1 | `unicode_confusable` | Unicode TR39 confusables | IN-COVERAGE (confusable map) |
| 2 | `zero_width` | Unicode zero-width / format chars | IN-COVERAGE (zero-width strip) |
| 3 | `digit_split` | spacing/sep obfuscation (PII) | IN-COVERAGE (ignore-whitespace digit match) |
| 4 | `fullwidth_compat` | Unicode NFKC compatibility / full-width forms | PARTIAL (NFKC handles full-width; combining marks uncertain) |
| 5 | `base64` | encoding obfuscation | OUT-OF-COVERAGE (no decoder) |
| 6 | `hex` | encoding obfuscation | OUT-OF-COVERAGE (no decoder) |
| 7 | `rot13` | substitution-cipher obfuscation | OUT-OF-COVERAGE (no decoder) |
| 8 | `leetspeak` | character-substitution obfuscation | OUT-OF-COVERAGE |
| 9 | `bidi_override` | Unicode bidirectional override (Trojan-Source-style) | OUT-OF-COVERAGE |
| 10 | `instruction_paraphrase` | indirect prompt-injection rephrasing | OUT-OF-COVERAGE (static denylist) |
| 11 | `chained` | composition of two of the above | MIXED |

The taxonomy is deliberately **broader than the redactor's rule set**: families 5–10 are
known a priori to have no corresponding redactor rule. We report results for all families,
including expected failures, and never sort families into pass/fail after seeing scores.

## 3. Generation
- Deterministic given a seed. The full census is generated under **20 seeds** (0–19) so
  across-seed sensitivity can be reported.
- Per (family × payload-class), parameters are sampled from declared ranges (e.g.,
  zero-width insertion count ~ Uniform(1, 6); confusable substitution density swept for
  dose–response). Parameters and ranges live in `generator.py`.
- A **benign control** split (no payload) measures literal removal of designated benign
  sentinel tokens. Its unit is not compatible with payload-case recall and is not used to
  compute precision or an F-score.
- Target census size: ~600–1000 payload-bearing cases + benign controls per seed-aggregate.

## 4. Exposure oracle (separate code path; not independent or dominant)
`oracle.py` shares no imports with `redaction.py`. It applies NFKD, a broader-but-different
confusable fold, removal of zero-width/format/bidirectional characters, whitespace
removal, case folding, and attempted
base64/hex/rot13 decoding. This is not a semantic superset of the redactor. In particular,
the redactor maps Greek alpha (`α`) to ASCII `a`, while the oracle does not; a repeated
Greek-alpha credential can therefore be redacted while the oracle fails to recognize the
canonical ASCII payload. `P` is **exposed** if, after normalization of the defense output,
either
(a) the canonical payload survives in full, or (b) a contiguous canonical substring of
length ≥ `ceil(0.7 · |P|)` survives (partial-mask recoverability). Otherwise **neutralized**.

Controls (asserted in `test_heldout.py`):
- **Positive control:** a base64-encoded payload that the redactor leaves intact MUST be
  flagged `exposed` by the oracle (checks that one decoder path; it does not establish
  general dominance).
- **Negative control:** benign text with no payload MUST yield no exposure (no phantom hits).

## 5. Defenses compared (same oracle applied to each output)
1. `naive` — exact-substring credential/PII redaction with **no normalization** (no NFKC,
   no confusable fold, no whitespace handling).
2. `perceptfence` — the repository `RedactionEngine` (the system under test).
3. `presidio` — **real Microsoft Presidio** (`presidio-analyzer` 2.2.359, spaCy
   `en_core_web_sm` 3.8.0 NER + predefined pattern recognizers), run offline; detected
   entity spans replaced with placeholders. This is the real-tool baseline, run on *our*
   corpus (not a reimplementation, not borrowed numbers).

## 6. Metrics (deterministic-census reporting; no population inference)
Per family and overall:
- **recall** = neutralized / payload-bearing (primary security metric).
- **benign-removal proxy** = designated benign sentinel tokens removed / designated
  benign sentinel tokens. It is reported separately from payload recall.
- Reported as **exact k/n counts** plus **across-seed spread** (min–max over the 20 seeds).
  Dose–response curves are reported for parameterized families.
- **Explicitly NOT reported:** precision, F-scores, population confidence intervals, or
  significance tests. Payload cases and benign tokens are incompatible observational
  units. The
  census is a deterministic stress-test of a generator, not a sample from a real-world
  threat population; any spread shown quantifies generator randomization only, never
  generalization to real screen-share sessions.
- Tables are split **IN-COVERAGE vs OUT-OF-COVERAGE**. The in-coverage block is a
  functional-correctness/regression check ("does it do what it claims"); only the
  out-of-coverage block and the real-Presidio comparison speak to robustness.

## 7. Honesty commitments (pre-declared)
- We expect and will report `recall < 1.0` overall; out-of-coverage families are expected
  failures and are reported as the system's characterized coverage boundary, not hidden.
- The in-coverage "win" over `naive` is acknowledged as partly tautological (the redactor
  was built for those families) and is never headlined as robustness.
- Residual generative circularity (generator, redactor, and oracle share authorship) is
  stated as a limitation; literature-informed families, a separate oracle code path,
  multi-seed sensitivity, and real third-party baseline are partial mitigations, not a
  claim of independence.

## 8. Protocol Amendment v2 (disclosed; 2026-06-24)

Post-registration harsh-PC, methodology, and venue-fit review
identified two defects in the v1 generator that distorted the out-of-coverage block.
Both are corrected here as a **disclosed, dated amendment** — not a silent retro-fit —
and the **entire census was regenerated** (all 20 std-lib seeds + 5 real-Presidio
seeds) after the change. Historical provenance is preserved by disclosure and by
git history: the v1 PROTOCOL + v1 CSVs remain in the commit log; this amendment and
the regenerated CSVs are separate, later commits.

1. **`bidi_override` operator was wrap-only (a scoring artifact).** v1 `_apply_bidi`
   wrapped the payload in a single RLO/PDI pair (`o + s + c`), leaving the credential
   contiguous, so the redactor's ASCII match still fired and the family scored a
   misleadingly high recall (1.000). v2 **interleaves** bidi/format controls
   (U+202A–U+202E, U+2066–U+2069) *inside* the payload (Trojan-Source style), so a
   contiguous match fails on the raw stream. This makes `bidi_override` an **honest
   out-of-coverage miss** for the NFKC-only redactor (which does not strip U+202A–E),
   while the oracle — which strips all Cf format characters — still recovers ground
   truth on this operator (the relevant positive control in `test_heldout.py` still
   passes). Expect `bidi_override`
   recall to **drop** for PerceptFence under v2; this is the correct, non-artifactual
   result.

2. **`chained` coverage label corrected `out` → `partial`.** The `chained` family
   composes `unicode_confusable` + `zero_width`, both of which are **in-coverage**
   operators (Section 2, rows 1–2). Labeling it `out` in v1 inflated the
   out-of-coverage aggregate. v2 labels it `partial`, consistent with the v1 PROTOCOL
   §2 row-11 text ("MIXED") and with the operators it actually uses. Block aggregates
   (in/partial/out/all) are recomputed accordingly.

No other operator, payload, oracle rule, seed count, or Presidio configuration changed.
The oracle remains import-separated from the redactor, but Section 4 now records why
import separation is not methodological independence or semantic dominance.

_Amended 2026-06-24, after v1 results were committed; full census regenerated under
real Microsoft Presidio on the pinned `requirements-eval.txt` stack._

## 9. Protocol Amendment v3 — paired cross-tool comparison (2026-07-09)

Late hostile review found that the manuscript compared the 20-seed PerceptFence
aggregate with a five-seed Presidio aggregate. That comparison was not paired even though
the seed subsets were nominally drawn from the same generator. Amendment v3 adds
`run_paired_presidio.py`, which runs the naive matcher, PerceptFence, and Presidio on each identical generated case for
seeds 0–4 and writes `eval/results/heldout_paired_presidio.csv` with exact denominators.

This is a deliberate superseding evaluation artifact, not a silent rewrite of the
original baseline at commit `81092d0`. The original payload counts in
`heldout_by_family.csv`, `heldout_overall.csv`, and `heldout_sensitivity.csv` remain
unchanged and continue to support only the 20-seed PerceptFence sensitivity analysis.
The invalid mixed-unit precision/F2 columns were removed from `heldout_overall.csv`, and
benign-token removal is now emitted separately in `heldout_benign_controls.csv`. All cross-tool values in the
candidate manuscript and coverage figure come from the v3 paired CSV. No generator,
payload, coverage label, oracle, redactor, or Presidio configuration changed for v3.

Reproduction command from the repository root after installing
`requirements-eval.txt`:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=eval/heldout:. python eval/heldout/run_paired_presidio.py \
  --seeds 0 1 2 3 4 \
  --output eval/results/heldout_paired_presidio.csv
```
